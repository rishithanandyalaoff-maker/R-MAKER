Your paycheck, decoded

Learning goal: read a US paycheck from gross pay to take-home, and know which pieces change for international students.

1. Gross pay is the starting number

Gross pay is your salary before anything is taken out. A $150,000 offer means about $12,500 per month before taxes and benefits.

2. Pre-tax bites come next

Many employers take traditional 401(k) contributions and health premiums before federal income tax. That lowers taxable income. Roth 401(k) contributions do not lower federal taxable income.

The employee 401(k) limit for this tax year is $24,500. The calculator shows that limit but defaults your contribution to $0 until you choose otherwise — we will not invent a savings rate for you.

3. Federal tax uses brackets

The US taxes income in layers. Your marginal rate is the rate on your next dollar. Your effective rate is total federal tax divided by income — usually lower.

Most people also get a standard deduction ($16,100 if single) before brackets apply. You do not need to memorize the table; the tool uses the public numbers.

4. FICA is Social Security + Medicare

FICA is separate from federal income tax. It funds Social Security and Medicare. Traditional 401(k) contributions usually do not reduce FICA. Pre-tax health premiums usually lower FICA too; traditional 401(k) contributions do not.

Visa note

F-1 / OPT nonresidents in their first five calendar years are often exempt from Social Security and Medicare. H-1B workers generally pay FICA. Check the box in the calculator only if that describes you, and confirm with your school’s international office.

5. State tax and bonuses

Some states (Texas, Florida, Washington, and others) have no wage income tax. The calculator defaults state tax to 0%. Bonuses are often withheld at a flat federal supplemental rate (22%) — that withholding is not always your final tax on the bonus.

Example

Worked example: $150,000 salary, single, $24,500 traditional 401(k), $3,000 health premiums, no state tax → about $7,760/month take-home. The calculator below starts with a $0 401(k), so it shows a higher take-home. Open optional details and enter $24,500 to match this example.

Key takeaways

  • Take-home is what remains after pre-tax benefits, federal tax, FICA, and state tax.
  • Marginal rate ≠ average rate.
  • Pre-tax health usually lowers FICA; traditional 401(k) does not.
  • FICA rules can differ on F-1/OPT vs H-1B.
