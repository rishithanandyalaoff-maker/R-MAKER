Why start now

Learning goal: see why starting early matters more than picking perfect investments.

1. Savings rate comes first

Early on, how much you keep matters more than the exact return. A higher savings rate builds the pile that later growth can compound.

2. Compounding needs time

Compounding means growth earns more growth. Time is the main ingredient. Waiting five years to start is usually costlier than picking a slightly worse fund.

3. Your independence number

A common teaching shortcut: financial independence is roughly 25× yearly spending. At $3,000/month ($36,000/year), that is about $900,000.

Key takeaways

  • Start with a savings habit, not stock tips.
  • Time in the market beats delayed perfection.
  • Independence ≈ 25× yearly spending.
